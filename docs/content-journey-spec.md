# The content journey: what the next post is for

Session 1 (Marketing). Asked for by the product owner on 31 Oct 2026: the studio should start the way a strong marketing
lead would. A new account opens with an introduction, not a listing, and every later post has a reason.

## The approach

A strong lead answers these in order, and the studio now does too:

1. **Where is the account?** New, building trust, growing, or back after a silence. This decides everything else.
2. **What is this post's job?** Introduce us, say why us, teach, show proof, show the team, or offer something.
3. **What is it about?** The brand, a topic, or a listing or product, whichever does that job.
4. **How do we say it?** The format with the best evidence: our own posts first, then competitors' clear winners, then
   rising trends. A trend or a competitor gives the shape, never the message. Company facts give the message.
5. **Did it hold up?** The review checks it, a person picks one, and the results feed step 4.

Before those five comes one question: **has something happened that matters more than the plan?** A moment goes first,
whatever the stage.

### Moments

| Moment | How it arrives | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| A new project | A project card confirmed in Knowledge in the last 14 days | Made once, from that card's facts |
| Something big happened (news, a law, the market) | A person types it with its facts, in the Run tab | Lasts 7 days unless a date is given. The facts are the person's, so nothing is invented about the news |

The studio does not decide by itself that the world's news is worth a post. A tragedy or a conflict is a human call, and
a brand that reacts wrongly is hurt more than one that stays quiet. The trend search can show what is rising; a person
turns it into a moment.

### The stages

| Stage | When | What the studio does |
|---|---|---|
| Not known yet | No posts can be seen and nobody has said whether the account is new | Asks. Zero posts with no Instagram connection is not the same as a new account |
| Introduce | Fewer than 9 posts | The first nine, below |
| Build trust | 9 to 29 posts | The usual mix of topics and listings (Setup → Rhythm) |
| Grow | 30 or more posts | The same mix, with the angles chosen from our own results |
| Comeback | Last post more than 45 days ago | One post that says we are back and what is coming, then the stage it was in |

### The first nine posts

| # | Job | About | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduce | The brand | Who we are and who we help. Every new visitor needs this first |
| 2 | Why us | The brand | What is different, before asking for anything |
| 3 | Teach | A topic | Useful to a viewer who will not buy today |
| 4 | Offer | A listing or product | The first sale, after three posts that gave something |
| 5 | Prove | The brand | Real results, only from confirmed facts with numbers |
| 6 | Teach | A topic | Back to being useful |
| 7 | Offer | A listing or product | |
| 8 | Team | The brand | The people, only from confirmed staff cards |
| 9 | Teach | A topic | |

A job with nothing true to say is skipped and the panel says why: no proof is better than invented proof. Introduce and
Why us are not skipped: without a brand name and "Who we are" the studio asks for them.

## Checklist (definition of done)

1. A brand-new account (Instagram connected, 0 posts, or a person said it is new) is told its first post is the
   introduction, and one click writes three introduction scripts from the brand profile and confirmed company facts.
2. With no posts visible and no answer, the Run tab asks; it never assumes the account is new.
3. The Run tab shows the stage, the next post's job and why, and the nine steps with what is done.
4. The plan follows the first nine, then the usual mix. A person can still run any listing at any time.
5. An approved script counts towards the nine (publishing may be off), each once.
6. An account silent for over 45 days is told to make a comeback post first.
7. Listing and product scripts also receive the confirmed company facts (at most 6, the most relevant first).
8. The review sends back a claim with a number ("15 years", "500 families", "98%") that no source supports.
9. The three scripts of a run come from three different ideas, when there are three.
10. Only a clear win is pinned to the research board: more than twice the square root of the account's average above it,
    the same rule Why they win uses.
11. Empty and error states: no brand name, no company facts, no topics, no listings. Each says what is missing.
12. A moment goes before everything else: the panel shows it first, the plan's first day takes it, and once its script is
    approved it is done. A moment needs facts (a sentence or two); a title alone is refused.

## Not in this piece

- The five listing angles are still property words in the code.
- Build trust and Grow use the existing mix; they have no fixed sequence of jobs.
- A paid or boosted post is not planned.
- A cloned human voice. HeyGen's presenter speaks with its avatar's voice, and the free video uses the computer's voice.
  A clone of a real person needs their recorded consent, like the avatar does.
- Finding moments in the news by itself. It needs the Meta key and a decision on what the studio may react to.
- The hidden logic of a competitor's winners stays limited to what Instagram gives: likes, comments, captions and covers.
  Watch time and saves are not available; a reel uploaded to the Marketing lead gives its speech, cuts and frames too.
